BLOOD PRESSURE MEASUREMENT
Blood pressure is measured in mmHg and recorded as systolic/diastolic. Accurate measurement requires the patient to sit quietly for 5 minutes, feet flat on the floor, arm supported at heart level. An appropriately sized cuff is essential (too small gives falsely high readings). Multiple readings on separate occasions are required for diagnosis of hypertension. Home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) and ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) provide valuable additional data. Normal blood pressure is <120/80 mmHg; elevated is 120-129/<80 mmHg; Stage 1 hypertension is 130-139/80-89 mmHg; Stage 2 hypertension is ≥140/90 mmHg.

BLOOD GLUCOSE TESTING
Blood glucose can be measured via fasting plasma glucose, random plasma glucose, or self-monitoring blood glucose (SMBG) using a glucometer. Fasting glucose requires no caloric intake for at least 8 hours. HbA1c reflects average blood glucose over 2-3 months and is used for diabetes diagnosis and monitoring. Target HbA1c for most patients with type 2 diabetes is <7% per ADA guidelines, individualized based on patient factors. Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) devices provide real-time glucose readings. SMBG frequency depends on medication regimen and glycemic control.

COLONOSCOPY
Colonoscopy is an endoscopic procedure that examines the entire large intestine and the end of the small intestine. It is used for colorectal cancer screening, evaluation of gastrointestinal symptoms, and removal of polyps (polypectomy). Current guidelines recommend screening starting at age 45 for average-risk individuals. Preparation involves bowel cleansing with a prescribed laxative solution the day before. The procedure is performed under sedation. Risks include perforation (<0.1%), bleeding (particularly post-polypectomy), and adverse reactions to sedation. A normal screening colonoscopy is repeated every 10 years; intervals depend on findings.

ECG (ELECTROCARDIOGRAM)
An ECG records the electrical activity of the heart using electrodes placed on the skin. It is a non-invasive, painless procedure taking approximately 10 minutes. It evaluates heart rate and rhythm, conduction abnormalities, signs of myocardial ischemia or infarction, chamber enlargement, and electrolyte disturbances. A standard 12-lead ECG provides views of the heart from multiple angles. Exercise stress testing (stress ECG) evaluates for coronary artery disease. Holter monitoring provides continuous 24-48 hour ECG recording for intermittent arrhythmia detection.

MRI (MAGNETIC RESONANCE IMAGING)
MRI uses magnetic fields and radio waves to create detailed images of organs and tissues. It is particularly useful for imaging the brain, spinal cord, joints, and soft tissues. It does not use ionizing radiation, making it safer for repeated imaging. Contraindications include certain metallic implants (pacemakers, some cochlear implants, certain metal clips), claustrophobia, and inability to remain still. Contrast-enhanced MRI uses gadolinium contrast agents; renal function should be assessed beforehand due to risk of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis in patients with severe renal impairment. The procedure typically takes 30-60 minutes.

VACCINATION
Vaccination stimulates the immune system to produce immunity to specific diseases. The recommended immunization schedule varies by age, health status, occupation, and geographic location. Adult vaccines include influenza (annually), COVID-19, Tdap (tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis), pneumococcal, shingles (herpes zoster for adults ≥50), and hepatitis A and B. Vaccines undergo rigorous safety testing before approval. Common side effects include soreness at the injection site, mild fever, and fatigue — signs of immune activation. Serious adverse events are rare but monitored through pharmacovigilance systems. Vaccine schedules should be discussed with a healthcare provider.
