TYPE 2 DIABETES MELLITUS
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. It accounts for approximately 90-95% of all diabetes cases worldwide. Risk factors include obesity, physical inactivity, family history, age over 45, and prior gestational diabetes. Symptoms include polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, fatigue, blurred vision, and slow wound healing. Diagnosis is established by fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL, 2-hour glucose ≥200 mg/dL during OGTT, HbA1c ≥6.5%, or random glucose ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms. Management includes lifestyle modification (diet, exercise, weight loss), oral antidiabetic agents (metformin as first-line), and insulin if needed. Complications include cardiovascular disease, nephropathy, retinopathy, neuropathy, and increased infection risk.

HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)
Hypertension is defined as persistent elevation of blood pressure ≥130/80 mmHg (ACC/AHA 2017 guidelines). It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, and kidney disease. Primary (essential) hypertension accounts for 90-95% of cases with no identifiable cause. Secondary hypertension may be due to renal disease, primary aldosteronism, sleep apnea, or thyroid disorders. Most patients are asymptomatic; hypertension is often detected incidentally. Lifestyle modifications include DASH diet, sodium restriction (<2.3g/day), regular aerobic exercise, weight management, and limiting alcohol. Pharmacological treatment includes thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and calcium channel blockers, selected based on comorbidities.

ASTHMA
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by variable airflow obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and underlying inflammation. It affects approximately 300 million people globally. Common triggers include allergens (dust mites, pet dander, pollen), respiratory infections, exercise, cold air, smoke, and stress. Symptoms include episodic wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough, often worse at night or early morning. Diagnosis is based on clinical history, spirometry showing reversible airflow obstruction (≥12% improvement in FEV1 post-bronchodilator), and peak flow variability. Treatment follows a step-up approach per GINA guidelines: short-acting beta-agonists (SABAs) for relief, inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) as controller therapy, and add-on therapies for severe asthma.

DEPRESSION (MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER)
Major depressive disorder (MDD) is characterized by persistent depressed mood and/or loss of interest lasting at least 2 weeks, accompanied by cognitive and physical symptoms. DSM-5 criteria require 5 or more symptoms including depressed mood, anhedonia, weight/appetite changes, sleep disturbance, psychomotor changes, fatigue, guilt/worthlessness, difficulty concentrating, and recurrent thoughts of death. It affects approximately 280 million people worldwide (WHO). Biological factors include genetic predisposition and neurotransmitter dysregulation. Treatment includes psychotherapy (CBT, IPT), pharmacotherapy (SSRIs as first-line), and in severe cases, electroconvulsive therapy. Integrated care and regular follow-up are essential.

GASTROESOPHAGEAL REFLUX DISEASE (GERD)
GERD occurs when gastric acid refluxes into the esophagus, causing symptoms or mucosal damage. Classic symptoms include heartburn and regurgitation, typically postprandial or when supine. Atypical manifestations include chronic cough, laryngitis, and chest pain. Risk factors include obesity, pregnancy, hiatal hernia, and certain medications. Diagnosis is primarily clinical; endoscopy is reserved for alarm symptoms or refractory cases. Lifestyle modifications include weight loss, elevating the head of bed, avoiding late meals, reducing alcohol and caffeine. Pharmacological treatment includes antacids, H2 receptor antagonists, and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs).

ANXIETY DISORDERS
Anxiety disorders are the most common mental health conditions, including generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, and specific phobias. GAD is characterized by excessive, uncontrollable worry about various topics for ≥6 months. Panic disorder involves recurrent unexpected panic attacks with persistent concern about future attacks. Physical symptoms include palpitations, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, and dizziness. Treatment includes cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), which is highly effective, and pharmacotherapy with SSRIs, SNRIs, or buspirone. Benzodiazepines may be used short-term for acute anxiety but carry risk of dependence.

HYPOTHYROIDISM
Hypothyroidism results from insufficient thyroid hormone production. The most common cause in developed countries is Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune). Symptoms include fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, depression, bradycardia, and myxedema in severe cases. Diagnosis is confirmed by elevated TSH with low free T4. Subclinical hypothyroidism presents with elevated TSH but normal free T4. Treatment is levothyroxine replacement therapy, with dose adjusted based on TSH levels. Monitoring every 6-12 months is recommended once stable.
